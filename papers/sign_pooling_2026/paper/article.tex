\abstract{Marginal sign restrictions stabilise sparse multi-output fits, but a
sign estimated one response at a time is noisy when observations are few. We pool marginal slopes across clusters of
responses, scale each pooled slope by a date-level sandwich variance that
retains contemporaneous response dependence, and use the resulting statistic
as a gate that restricts a coefficient's direction, excludes the coefficient,
or leaves it free before a sparse-group Lasso fit. For fixed dimensions we give
sufficient conditions under which the gate recovers the direction of every
nonzero coefficient, a checkable condition that implies the required
marginal-sign agreement, and the precision ratio $\sqrt{m/\{1+(m-1)\rho_e\}}$ of a slope pooled over $m$
responses with marginal-residual correlation $\rho_e$, which a Monte Carlo
check confirms to within one percent. In simulations with learned clusters
and training-only tuning, pooling raises the proportion of correct active
signs at a null-cell retention of $0.10$ by $0.030$ when cluster members share
supports and directions, and lowers it by $0.066$ and $0.134$ under mixed
directions and partial supports. With exclusion it raises test $R^2$ in the
coherent design from $0.614$ to $0.626$, level with cooperative Lasso and
UniLasso. In repeated nested validation on yeast expression quantitative trait
locus data, excluding failed gates lowers median-gene $R^2$ from $0.073$ to
$0.035$, releasing them leaves it at $0.073$, and replacing an independent-cell
variance by the date-level variance newly excludes $32.7$ percent of
coefficient cells without improving prediction. The gate should restrict
directions. Exclusion pays only when the grouping is coherent.}

\section{Introduction} \label{sec:intro}

In multi-output regression, a coefficient's sign describes the direction of a
conditional association. Examples include whether a factor loading increases
portfolio exposure \citep{sepp2026rosaa} and whether a genetic marker is associated
with higher or lower expression after adjusting for other markers. A marginal
regression can estimate a different direction when predictors are correlated.
Pooling marginal evidence across responses can improve precision, but it does
not resolve that distinction. We study when a pooled marginal sign is a useful
restriction for a subsequent sparse regression, and when it excludes a
nonzero coefficient or constrains it in the wrong direction.

% [CLAUDE-DRAFT begin: literature and substitution]
The univariate-guided least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) of
\citet{Chatterjee+Hastie+Tibshirani:2025} is the incumbent construction. It
restricts each coefficient to the sign of its own marginal regression and fits
a nonnegative second stage on univariate features. The pooled gate replaces
the per-response sign by a cluster-level sign and adds a screening step. The
cost of switching is a response partition and a threshold, both of which must
be tuned. Sign constraints alone regularise a regression:
\citet{Meinshausen:2013} and \citet{Slawski+Hein:2013} show that sign- and
nonnegativity-constrained least squares recover sparse signals without a
penalty under conditions on the design. Those results take the signs as given,
whereas the gate supplies them. The cooperative LASSO of
\citet{Chiquet+Grandvalet+Charbonnier:2012} encourages sign coherence through a
penalty that learns the group direction during fitting and can set groups to
zero. We instead fix a permitted direction, or exclude a coefficient, before
the penalized fit. Group and multivariate sparse group penalties
\citep{Yuan+Lin:2006,Obozinski+Taskar+Jordan:2010,Li+Nan+Zhu:2015} encode
coefficient groups, multivariate regression with covariance estimation
\citep{Rothman+Levina+Zhu:2010} and structured penalties for eQTL mapping
\citep{Peng+etal:2010,Kim+Xing:2012} share information across responses
through the loss or the penalty, and adaptive penalties
\citep{Zou:2006,Wang+Leng:2008} use pilot magnitudes to reweight shrinkage.
Marginal screening \citep{Fan+Lv:2008} supplies the idea of filtering weak
marginal evidence before fitting. We compare the complete fitted estimators of
these families as well as their preliminary screens.
% [CLAUDE-DRAFT end]

% [CLAUDE-DRAFT begin: contribution]
We make three contributions. We develop a dependence-aware pooled sign gate
whose statistic scales the cluster-pooled slope by a date-level score
variance, and whose failure can either exclude a coefficient or leave it
free. We establish, for fixed dimensions, sufficient conditions under which the
gate retains the correct direction on every nonzero coefficient
(Proposition~\ref{prop:gate}), give a checkable condition on predictor
covariances and coefficients that implies the required marginal-sign
agreement (Lemma~\ref{lem:leakage}), and derive the precision ratio
$\sqrt{m/\{1+(m-1)\rho_e\}}$ of pooling $m$ responses whose marginal
residuals have correlation $\rho_e$, which a Monte Carlo check confirms to
within one percent (Fig.~\ref{fig:precision}). We show in which response
structures the gate helps and in which it harms. At a null-cell retention of
$0.10$, pooling changes the proportion of correct active signs by $+0.030$ in
a coherent-group design and by $-0.066$ and $-0.134$ under mixed directions
and partial supports (Fig.~\ref{fig:gate-tradeoff}). In repeated nested
validation on yeast eQTL data, excluding failed gates lowers median-gene
$R^2$ from $0.073$ to $0.035$ while releasing them leaves it at $0.073$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:eqtl-genes}). The theory concerns gate directions on nonzero
coefficients and not the support of the fitted coefficient matrix. The
experiments establish no general predictive advantage over cooperative Lasso,
multivariate sparse group Lasso or UniLasso.
% [CLAUDE-DRAFT end]

We evaluate complete fitted estimators in two simulation families. The first
compares nine methods across six low- and moderate-signal designs. The second
uses 40 paired replications of five recognizable-group designs, jointly tunes
cut, mixing, threshold and penalty where applicable, and measures actual
partition recovery. The yeast eQTL analysis repeats preprocessing within
nested validation and measures prediction, partition stability, coefficient
selection and conditional direction agreement. Extended results and proof
details are supplied in Online Resource~1, and Online Resource~2 contains the
replication source and numerical evidence. The method is implemented in the
open-source Python package
\texttt{factorlasso}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/ArturSepp/factorlasso}}
The broader software interface and portfolio applications are described in an
unpublished companion manuscript by the same authors.

Section~\ref{sec:methods} develops the method. Section~\ref{sec:theory} states the
on-support gate result, the sufficient condition for marginal-sign agreement
and the pooling precision calculation. Section~\ref{sec:simulation} reports the
simulation study. Section~\ref{sec:eqtl} applies the method to yeast eQTL data.
Section~\ref{sec:reproduction} describes computation and reproducibility, and
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the implications.

\section{Methodology} \label{sec:methods}

The procedure takes a training matrix of predictors and responses, a response
partition or clustering rule, a gate threshold $\tau$, a variance convention,
and penalty parameters $(\lambda,\alpha)$. Its outputs are
the pooled slopes, their screening statistics, the constraint matrix, and the
fitted coefficient matrix. These are distinct evaluation targets.
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Select complete observations and estimate any
	centering, scaling and predictor-screening transformations on the training data.
	\item Supply response clusters or construct them from the training responses
	using the stated distance, linkage and cut rule.
	\item Compute each cluster--predictor marginal slope and its date-score
	variance. The archived independent-cell variance is a separate comparator.
	\item Retain the slope's direction when its statistic passes $\tau$. Otherwise
	exclude that predictor for all cluster members by fixing the coefficients to
	zero, or leave the cells unrestricted under the release variant. Record any
	explicitly unrestricted cells separately.
	\item Form the chosen hierarchical-clustering group LASSO (HCGL) or
	factor-clustering group LASSO (FCGL) penalty and solve the constrained
	optimization problem. Select hyperparameters using training-only validation,
	repeating Steps 1--4 within each inner training fold, then refit on the full
	outer training sample.
\end{enumerate}
Held-out responses are used only for evaluation. The principal estimator uses
complete, equally weighted observations. An optional masked and weighted gate
is described separately in Online Resource~1, Section~\ref{app:weighted-gate}.
The remainder of this section defines each step.

\subsection{Multi-response setup} \label{sec:setup}

Let $\bm{Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times N}$ collect $N$ responses observed over $T$
observations, and let $\bm{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times M}$ collect $M$ predictors
observed over the same observations. The multi-response regression model is
%
\begin{equation} \label{eq:setup}
	\bm{Y} \;=\; \bm{X}\, \bm{\beta}^\top + \bm{\varepsilon},
\end{equation}
%
where $\bm{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times M}$ is the coefficient matrix and
$\bm{\varepsilon} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times N}$ is the residual. The estimator
centers the columns of $\bm{Y}$ and $\bm{X}$ before fitting and recovers the
intercept afterward. We write $\bm{x}_j \in \mathbb{R}^T$ for the $j$-th
predictor column, $\bm{y}_k \in \mathbb{R}^T$ for the $k$-th response column, and
$\beta_{kj}$ for the $(k, j)$ entry. We partition the $N$ responses into clusters
$\{\mathcal{C}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{C}_G\}$, where each
$\mathcal{C}_g \subseteq \{1, \ldots, N\}$ is a set of response indices and
$|\mathcal{C}_g|$ its size. We write $\mathcal{C}(k)$ for the cluster containing
response $k$.

\subsection{Cluster-pooled marginal slopes} \label{sec:univ-slope}

For a response cluster $\mathcal C$ and predictor $j$, the pooled no-intercept
least-squares slope is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slope-cluster}
 \hat\beta_{j,\mathcal C}
 =\frac{\bm x_j^\top\sum_{k\in\mathcal C}\bm y_k}
 {|\mathcal C|\,\bm x_j^\top\bm x_j}.
\end{equation}
It is the slope of the cluster-mean response on $\bm x_j$, extending the
single-response marginal construction of
\citet{Chatterjee+Hastie+Tibshirani:2025}. Broadcasting it gives every cluster
member the same permitted direction. Broadcasting does not establish that their
partial coefficients share that direction. Positive response correlation is
insufficient, and a cluster can mix active and inactive responses.

\subsection{The pooled sign gate} \label{sec:gate}

The pooled sign gate uses the date-score statistic
$t_{j,\mathcal C}=\hat\beta_{j,\mathcal C}/\sqrt{\widehat V_{j,\mathcal C}}$,
with the dependence-aware score scale defined in
Section~\ref{sec:weighted-gate}. The resulting sign matrix is
%
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gate}
	s_{kj} \;=\;
	\begin{cases}
		\operatorname{sign}\bigl(\hat{\beta}_{j, \mathcal{C}(k)}\bigr), &
		|t_{j, \mathcal{C}(k)}| \;\geq\; \tau, \\
		0, & |t_{j, \mathcal{C}(k)}| \;<\; \tau,
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}
%
where $\tau\geq0$ is a tuning parameter. Under exclusion, a failed gate sets
$s_{kj}=0$ and therefore forces $\beta_{kj}=0$ for every $k\in\mathcal C$ on
predictor $j$. Under release, a failed gate leaves those cells unrestricted.
Exclusion can remove a genuine signal. A retained marginal sign can disagree
with a partial coefficient and make that coefficient's true value infeasible,
and a hard half-space still permits a nonzero estimate in that incorrect
direction.

A fixed threshold supplies neither multiplicity control nor a guarantee of
correct partial signs. Predictive tuning selects the threshold inside the
training loop. The eQTL analysis instead fixes $\tau=2$ and tunes only the
penalty level. Null-cell retention is distinct from the fraction of reported
signs that are false.

\subsection{Response dependence in the gate}\label{sec:weighted-gate}

The principal estimator uses complete observations with equal temporal weights.
Write $\widehat b_j=\hat\beta_{j,\mathcal C}$ for the pooled slope defined above.
Centering and scaling are learned on the training sample before clustering or
pooling. For a cluster $\mathcal C$ of size $m$, define
\begin{equation}
D_j=m\sum_{t=1}^T x_{tj}^2,\qquad
u_{tj}=x_{tj}\sum_{k\in\mathcal C}(y_{tk}-\widehat b_jx_{tj}),\qquad
\widehat V_j=\frac{T}{T-1}\frac{\sum_tu_{tj}^2}{D_j^2}.
\label{eq:date-score}
\end{equation}
This score-sandwich construction retains contemporaneous marginal-residual
covariance \citep{White:1980,Liang+Zeger:1986,CameronMiller2015}. For a
prespecified cluster and fixed analysis scales, it estimates the pooled slope's
asymptotic variance under independent and identically distributed (iid) dates.
When column standard deviations are learned from the same sample, it retains a
positive score-scale limit under those conditions, which is sufficient for the
on-support gate result, but we do not assert that it estimates the full sampling
variance of the normalized slope. We use $t_j=\widehat b_j/\sqrt{\widehat V_j}$
in Equation~\eqref{eq:gate}. A predictor with $D_j=0$ provides no slope evidence
and is excluded. The finite-sample multiplier $T/(T-1)$ is a screening
convention and not a claim of an exact Student distribution. Serial dependence
would require an additional heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent
(HAC) or block treatment.

The fitted protocols require at least two training observations and nonconstant
training columns. If $\widehat V_j=0$, a nonzero slope is assigned
$t_j=\operatorname{sign}(\widehat b_j)\,\infty$ and a zero slope gives $t_j=0$.
For tuning, $\tau=+\infty$ is defined separately as complete gate failure,
including these noiseless cases. The pooled slopes themselves are unchanged.

For a fixed cluster, repeating every member $c$ times multiplies $D_j$ and
$u_{tj}$ by $c$ and leaves the slope and variance unchanged. Duplicating only
one member of a heterogeneous cluster can change the estimand, and reclustering
after duplication is outside this invariance statement. The balanced
variance calculation in Section~\ref{sec:theory} uses marginal-regression
residual correlation, not raw response correlation. The archived
independent-cell comparator divides the pooled residual sum of squares by
$(Tm-1)D_j$, which treats the $m$ member residuals within a date as
independent. Section~\ref{sec:theory} quantifies how far that comparator
overstates precision.

\subsection{Cluster discovery} \label{sec:hcgl}

The partition can be supplied externally or learned from training responses.
The fitted experiments use Ward linkage on $D_{kl}=\sqrt{2(1-C_{kl})}$,
where $\bm C=\operatorname{corr}(\bm Y)$. These are Euclidean distances between
centered response vectors normalized to unit length when correlations use a
common observation grid \citep{Ward:1963,Murtagh+Legendre:2014}. Arbitrary
pairwise-missing correlations need not have this geometry. A cut fraction
$\kappa$ is applied to the final Ward merge height. The endpoints are
implemented explicitly as all singletons and full pooling. Intermediate cuts,
or an externally specified partition, need not yield coherent partial signs.

The first benchmark fixes $\kappa=0.5$, the learned-group study tunes it inside
training, and the eQTL baseline fixes $\kappa=0.7$. The companion software
manuscript's historical examples use a different correlation-distance
heuristic, and their partitions are not interchangeable with those here.

\subsection{Sign-constrained estimation} \label{sec:sgl-sign}

The derived signs constrain a multi-response sparse-group LASSO estimator
\citep{Simon+etal:2013}. Let $\mathcal U$ be a specified set of unrestricted
cells. On its complement the sign matrix has entries in $\{-1,0,+1\}$, and
Equation~\eqref{eq:gate} defines the constraint set
%
\begin{equation} \label{eq:sgl-sign-constraint}
	\mathcal{K}(\bm{S}) =
	\left\{\bm{\beta} :
	\begin{aligned}
	 \beta_{kj} &= 0 &&\text{if } (k,j)\notin\mathcal U,\ s_{kj}=0,\\
	 s_{kj}\beta_{kj} &\geq0 &&\text{if } (k,j)\notin\mathcal U,\
	 s_{kj}\in\{-1,+1\}
	\end{aligned}\right\}.
\end{equation}
%
A gated-out cell is pinned to zero, and a signed cell is held in the matching
half-space. The pooled exclusion procedure has $\mathcal U=\varnothing$. The
release variant moves the failed-gate cells into $\mathcal U$. An entirely
unrestricted ablation takes $\mathcal U$ to be all cells, removing both zero-pins
and sign half-spaces. Merely setting $\tau=0$ keeps sign restrictions and is not
an unrestricted fit. The software represents $\mathcal U$ by \texttt{NaN}
entries. With all slopes and penalty weights held fixed after preprocessing,
the HCGL estimator solves
%
\begin{align}
	\hat{\bm{\beta}} \;=\; \arg\min_{\bm{\beta} \in \mathcal{K}(\bm{S})} \;&
	\Biggl\{
	\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t = 1}^{T}
	\bigl\| \bm{y}_{t} - \bm{\beta}\, \bm{x}_{t} \bigr\|_2^2
	\;+\; \lambda\, (1 - \alpha) \sum_{g = 1}^{G} q_g\,
	\sum_{k \in \mathcal{C}_g} A_k
	\bigl\| \bm{\beta}_{k, \cdot} - \bm{\beta}^{0}_{k, \cdot} \bigr\|_2
	\notag \\
	& \qquad +\; \lambda\, \alpha \sum_{k = 1}^{N} \sum_{j = 1}^{M} W_{kj}
	\bigl| \beta_{kj} - \beta^{0}_{kj} \bigr|
	\Biggr\}, \label{eq:sgl-sign}
\end{align}
%
where $\lambda\geq0$ is the penalty strength, $\alpha\in[0,1]$ mixes the group
and cell penalties, and $\bm\beta^0$ is a specified target matrix, zero unless
stated otherwise. The group multiplier is
$q_g=\sqrt{|\mathcal C_g|/G}$. The software also offers the
$q_g=\sqrt{|\mathcal C_g|}$ convention, and experiments must identify a change
from the normalized convention used here. The HCGL group norm runs over
predictors within a response. Conditional on the partition, signs and weights,
its objective and constraints separate across responses. Clusters affect
shared preprocessing and the multiplier, not a joint response norm. With
$\alpha=1$ the estimator is a sign-constrained Lasso \citep{Tibshirani:1996}
with cluster-pooled signs, which is the case the learned-group tuning selects
most often (Section~\ref{sec:learned-validation}).

The factor-clustering group LASSO (FCGL) instead minimizes the same loss and
cell penalty with group term
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fcgl-penalty}
	\lambda(1-\alpha)\sum_{g=1}^G q_g
	\sum_{j=1}^M A_{gj}
	\bigl\|\bm\beta_{\mathcal C_g,j}
	-\bm\beta^0_{\mathcal C_g,j}\bigr\|_2 .
\end{equation}
This norm couples responses within each cluster--predictor block. HCGL and
FCGL use the same gate but are different fitted estimators.

With zero target and no sign restrictions, FCGL is the disjoint-block
special case of the multivariate sparse group LASSO objective in
\citet[Equation~(2)]{Li+Nan+Zhu:2015}. Set their coefficient matrix to
$\bm B=\bm\beta^\top$ and their groups to
$\mathcal G=\{\bm B_{j,\mathcal C_g}:j=1,\ldots,M,\ g=1,\ldots,G\}$.
Dividing our objective by two gives their $1/(2T)$ loss normalization,
with cell penalties $\lambda_{jk}=\lambda\alpha W_{kj}/2$ and group
penalties $\lambda_{(g,j)}=\lambda(1-\alpha)q_gA_{gj}/2$.
For singleton blocks the norm is an absolute value and its penalty can be
absorbed into the cell penalty. The unconstrained FCGL comparator therefore
implements this group structure within their objective family, without their
more general overlapping or nested group constructions or their original
tuning protocol. Our additional operation is the construction
of restrictions from pooled marginal evidence.

All fitted comparisons use $\bm\beta^0=0$ and
$W_{kj}=A_k=A_{gj}=1$. The optional target and adaptive-weight construction is
specified in Online Resource~1, Section~\ref{app:adaptive}, and is not validated
by these experiments. We claim no support-recovery or oracle distribution for
the fitted estimators. Online Resource~1, Section~\ref{app:computation}, gives
the implementation and numerical checks.

\section{On-support gate signs and pooling precision} \label{sec:theory}

% [CLAUDE-DRAFT begin: formal statement moved from Online Resource 1]
The result below concerns the sign matrix produced by the gate before
coefficient estimation. Let $(\bm x_t,\bm y_t)$ denote population-centered
observations with $\bm y_t=\bm\beta_{\mathrm{true}}\bm x_t+\bm\varepsilon_t$.
For a nonempty response subset $\mathcal C$ with $m=|\mathcal C|$ and
$\bar y_{t,\mathcal C}=m^{-1}\sum_{k\in\mathcal C}y_{tk}$, define the
population pooled slope, its marginal residual and its score scale,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:population-objects}
 b_{j,\mathcal C}=\frac{E[x_{tj}\bar y_{t,\mathcal C}]}{E[x_{tj}^2]},\qquad
 \bar r_{t,j,\mathcal C}=\bar y_{t,\mathcal C}-b_{j,\mathcal C}x_{tj},\qquad
 \omega_{j,\mathcal C}^2
 =\frac{E[x_{tj}^2\bar r_{t,j,\mathcal C}^{\,2}]}{E[x_{tj}^2]^2}.
\end{equation}
A pair $(j,\mathcal C)$ is relevant if $\beta_{\mathrm{true},kj}\ne0$ for at
least one $k\in\mathcal C$. Let $\mathfrak C$ be a deterministic finite family
of response subsets. The conditions are:
\begin{description}
 \item[(A1)] The dates $(\bm x_t,\bm\varepsilon_t)$ are iid with
 $E[\bm x_t]=\bm0$ and $E[\bm\varepsilon_t\mid\bm x_t]=\bm0$. The
 matrix $\bm\beta_{\mathrm{true}}$ and the dimensions $N,M$ do not vary with $T$.
 Response errors within a date may be dependent.
 \item[(A2)] All components of $\bm x_t$ and $\bm\varepsilon_t$ have finite
 fourth moments, $\bm\Sigma_F=E[\bm x_t\bm x_t^\top]$ is positive definite,
 and standardized columns have positive population variance. Only complete
 observations are used.
 \item[(A3)] The supplied or estimated partition $\widehat{\mathcal P}_T$
 satisfies $\Pr(\widehat{\mathcal P}_T\subseteq\mathfrak C)\to1$, and within
 every $\mathcal C\in\mathfrak C$ all nonzero $\beta_{\mathrm{true},kj}$ share
 one sign for each $j$.
 \item[(A4)] For constants $\delta>0$ and $0<c<C<\infty$, every relevant pair
 with $\mathcal C\in\mathfrak C$ has $|b_{j,\mathcal C}|\geq\delta$ and
 $c\leq\omega_{j,\mathcal C}^2\leq C$.
 \item[(A5)] The gate uses the complete-data, equal-weight statistic of
 Equation~\eqref{eq:date-score} with a deterministic threshold
 $\tau_T\geq0$, $\tau_T=o(\sqrt T)$.
 \item[(A6)] For every relevant $(j,\mathcal C)$ with
 $\mathcal C\in\mathfrak C$ and every nonzero $\beta_{\mathrm{true},kj}$ in
 that cluster, $\operatorname{sign}(b_{j,\mathcal C})
 =\operatorname{sign}(\beta_{\mathrm{true},kj})$.
\end{description}
Condition (A3) is an assumption about the clustering procedure under the
data-generating law. Corollary~1 in Online Resource~1,
Section~\ref{app:theory}, supplies sufficient population Ward and cut-margin
conditions for it, and a coherent Gaussian block model satisfies them.
Condition (A6) is the assumption that carries the result, and the lemma below
makes it checkable.

\begin{proposition}[correct gate signs on the support]\label{prop:gate}
Let $\widehat{\bm S}_T$ be the sign matrix of Equation~\eqref{eq:gate} with no
unrestricted cells. Under (A1)--(A6),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gate-main-result}
	\Pr\!\left\{\widehat S_{T,kj}
	=\operatorname{sign}(\beta_{\mathrm{true},kj})
	\text{ for every }\beta_{\mathrm{true},kj}\ne0\right\}\longrightarrow1 .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
The proof is in Online Resource~1, Section~\ref{app:theory}. Its steps are a
law of large numbers for the pooled slope and for $T\widehat V_{j,\mathcal C}$,
whose limit $\omega_{j,\mathcal C}^2$ is positive by (A4), and a union bound
over the finite family $\mathfrak C$, which controls every admissible cluster
simultaneously and so allows clusters estimated from the same sample. The
proposition does not assert that $\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{\bm\beta}_T)$
equals the true support, provides no oracle distribution for the penalized
estimator, and is not a high-dimensional result.

Under (A1) and (A2) the population pooled slope is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marginal-leakage-main}
 b_{j,\mathcal C}
 =\frac{1}{m\,\Sigma_{F,jj}}
 \sum_{k\in\mathcal C}\sum_{\ell=1}^M
 \beta_{\mathrm{true},k\ell}\Sigma_{F,j\ell},
\end{equation}
so a marginal sign aggregates the own effects $\beta_{\mathrm{true},kj}$ with
leakage from every other predictor correlated with $\bm x_j$. The lemma bounds
the leakage.

\begin{lemma}[a sufficient condition for marginal-sign agreement]\label{lem:leakage}
Assume (A1), (A2) and the coherence part of (A3) for a relevant pair
$(j,\mathcal C)$, with common sign $s\in\{-1,+1\}$ on its nonzero
$\beta_{\mathrm{true},kj}$. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leakage-condition}
 \Sigma_{F,jj}\sum_{k\in\mathcal C}|\beta_{\mathrm{true},kj}|
 \;>\;
 \sum_{k\in\mathcal C}\sum_{\ell\ne j}
 |\beta_{\mathrm{true},k\ell}|\,|\Sigma_{F,j\ell}| ,
\end{equation}
then $\operatorname{sign}(b_{j,\mathcal C})=s$ and
$|b_{j,\mathcal C}|$ is at least the difference of the two sides divided by
$m\,\Sigma_{F,jj}$. Condition (A6) holds for every relevant pair that
satisfies Equation~\eqref{eq:leakage-condition}, and the slope part of (A4)
holds with $\delta$ equal to the smallest of these lower bounds.
\end{lemma}
The proof is a triangle inequality applied to
Equation~\eqref{eq:marginal-leakage-main} and is given in Online Resource~1,
Section~\ref{app:theory}. With uncorrelated predictors the right-hand side is
zero and the condition holds for every relevant coherent pair. With correlated
predictors it requires the cluster-summed own effect to dominate the
cluster-summed leakage, a condition of the same type as, but distinct from,
the irrepresentable condition for Lasso sign consistency of
\citet{Zhao+Yu:2006} and the screening conditions of \citet{Fan+Lv:2008}.
The condition can be evaluated on any simulation design. It cannot be verified
on the eQTL data, where $\bm\beta_{\mathrm{true}}$ is unknown.
% [CLAUDE-DRAFT end]

Pooling precision can be calculated separately for a prespecified cluster.
For this variance calculation the analysis scales are fixed in advance.
Write $a_{kj}=\operatorname{Cov}(x_{tj},y_{tk})/
\operatorname{Var}(x_{tj})$ for its member marginal slopes and
$r_{tk}^{(j)}=y_{tk}-a_{kj}x_{tj}$ for the population-centered marginal
residuals. Their average slope is
$b_{j,\mathcal C}=m^{-1}\sum_{k\in\mathcal C}a_{kj}$, where
$m=|\mathcal C|$. Under linear conditional means, independent dates and
conditional residual covariance
$\operatorname{Cov}(\bm r_{t,\mathcal C}^{(j)}\mid x_{tj})=\bm\Omega_j$
constant over dates, centered ordinary least squares (OLS) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pooling-variance}
	\operatorname{Var}(\widehat b_{j,\mathcal C}\mid\bm x_j)
	=\frac{\bm1^\top\bm\Omega_j\bm1}
	{m^2\,\bm x_j^\top\bm x_j}.
\end{equation}
This follows by writing the slope error as
$(m\,\bm x_j^\top\bm x_j)^{-1}
\sum_t x_{tj}\sum_{k\in\mathcal C}r_{tk}^{(j)}$ and summing independent
date variances. Heterogeneous member slopes do not add a separate variance
term to their average, but they do change the signal being estimated.

If the marginal residual variances are all $\sigma_j^2$ and their correlations
are all $\rho_e$, Equation~\eqref{eq:pooling-variance} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pooling-design-effect}
	\frac{\sigma_j^2\{1+(m-1)\rho_e\}}
	{m\,\bm x_j^\top\bm x_j}.
\end{equation}
When member slopes also have the same magnitude and direction, the ratio of
pooled to per-response signal-to-noise is
$\sqrt{m/\{1+(m-1)\rho_e\}}$. It is $\sqrt m$ for independent marginal
residuals and one for identical residuals. For fixed $m$, both procedures have
root-$T$ order, and the ratio is a precision constant. Unequal effects, unequal
variances or partial support sharing need not produce this gain and can dilute
the pooled signal.

Fig.~\ref{fig:precision} checks Equation~\eqref{eq:pooling-design-effect} and
the two variance estimators by Monte Carlo. One predictor with $T=80$ is held
fixed, $m\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}$ responses share the slope $0.2$, and their
residuals are equicorrelated with $\rho_e\in\{0,0.3,0.6,0.9\}$, over 20,000

The covariance in this calculation is that of marginal regression residuals,
not of the full-model errors or the raw responses. Even independent full-model
errors can leave correlated marginal residuals through omitted predictors.
Equation~\eqref{eq:date-score} aggregates empirical marginal-residual scores
without replacing them by a raw-response correlation adjustment. Its positive
score-scale limit supports Proposition~\ref{prop:gate}, including the specified
sample standardization. A fixed gate threshold does not provide multiplicity
control, and dependence-aware standard errors cannot correct a marginal sign
that differs from the corresponding partial sign.

\section{Simulation study} \label{sec:simulation}

We distinguish preliminary gate signs, fitted selection and prediction. Both
studies use independent test samples and training-only validation. Online
Resource~1 gives complete protocols and all coefficient, support and sensitivity
results. Intervals describe paired Monte Carlo variation. They do not adjust
for multiple comparisons or the disclosed grid extensions.

\subsection{Fitted-estimator benchmark}\label{sec:fitted-benchmark}

Six designs each use 20 paired seeds, 80 training and 1,000 test observations,
12 predictors and 12 responses. The baseline has three equal clusters, shared
supports and signs, independent Gaussian predictors/errors and population
$R^2=0.30$. Variants introduce weaker signals, partial supports, mixed signs,
correlated predictors/errors, or unequal groups and error scales. Four matched
HCGL variants differ in their restrictions (unconstrained, singleton/exclude,
pooled/exclude and pooled/release). Comparators are unconstrained FCGL,
cooperative Lasso, ordinary Lasso and full UniLasso with its leave-one-out
univariate features and nonnegative second stage, and a known-group HCGL fit is
an oracle diagnostic. HCGL/FCGL fix $\alpha=0.5$ and the estimated partition
uses cut $0.5$. Lasso selects one common penalty fraction across responses
rather than tuning each response separately. Three-fold training validation
repeats preprocessing and jointly selects the penalty and applicable threshold
from fourteen penalty fractions of each method's training-only zero-solution
scale and the signed thresholds $\{0,0.75,1.5,2.5,3,6,\infty\}$. The grids were
extended after inspecting boundary selections, using the same seeds and folds,
and no test outcome or coefficient truth selects a candidate. Online
Resource~1, Sections~\ref{app:fitted-protocol} and~\ref{app:estimator-metrics},
specify every design and report every result, including independent-test
mean squared errors (MSE) in Table~\ref{tab:fitted-prediction}.

This benchmark barely pools. The estimated partitions have a mean of $10.15$
groups in the baseline, $12$ under weak signal and $3$ in the correlated
case, out of 12 responses. Pooling has no general predictive advantage over
unconstrained HCGL. In the correlated case its paired MSE loss is $0.01568$
(Monte Carlo standard error $0.00641$, paired $t_{19}$ interval
$[0.00226,0.02909]$), and releasing failed gates gives MSE $0.795$ against
$0.797$ unconstrained and $0.812$ with exclusion. Known groups help in the
baseline but harm prediction under mixed signs. The correlated case also
shows why fitted flip rates alone mislead. Excluded HCGL has zero observed
fitted sign flips, yet pins $25.6\%$ of active coefficients to zero and
assigns an incorrect gate direction to $12.8\%$ of active cells. Synthetic
support summaries use tolerance $10^{-4}$ in standardized units, following an
independent solver audit. Changing from $10^{-6}$ alters some recovery
proportions by up to five percentage points, and leaves prediction and
unthresholded coefficient error unaffected.

\subsection{Learned pooling in identifiable response groups}\label{sec:learned-validation}

To examine genuinely learned groups, five designs each use 40 paired seeds,
80 training observations, 1,000 independent test observations, 12 predictors
and three response blocks of size four. Two strong shared anchor predictors
and a weaker third predictor create a favorable population signal share of
$0.65$. Mixed-direction and partial-support designs alter the third effect, and
two further designs add predictor or within-block error correlation. This is
a diagnostic grouped regime, not an estimate of the yeast signal strength.
Online Resource~1, Section~\ref{app:learned-protocol}, gives the construction.

The eight feasible methods use zero targets and nonadaptive weights.
Three-fold inner validation jointly selects all applicable combinations of
cut $\{0,0.175,0.35,0.5,0.7,0.85,1\}$, mixing
$\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$, threshold $\{0,0.75,1.5,3,6,\infty\}$
and the fourteen penalty fractions. Lasso/UniLasso tune only their penalty, and
cooperative Lasso also tunes the cut. These final grids include natural domain
endpoints and followed two disclosed extensions after boundary diagnostics.
All preprocessing is fitted inside training, and coefficient truth is used only
for evaluation. A fresh cross-validation reproduction recomputes every
candidate loss. Finite grid resolution and selection variability remain.

A paired reference fixes $(\kappa,\alpha,\tau)=(0.5,0.5,1.5)$ and tunes only
the penalty. One-factor sensitivity changes one setting at a time. At this
cut the coherent groups are recovered exactly (mean adjusted Rand index one),
and mixed directions give mean index $0.617$ and $5.075$ learned groups. Group
recovery and suitability for a common sign restriction are different targets,
and jointly tuned procedures may select different partitions.

\section{Application to yeast eQTL data} \label{sec:eqtl}

Expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) relate genetic markers to gene
expression. The yeast data provide many correlated responses with uncertain grouping and
more candidate predictors than observations, and the same cross is the
benchmark of \citet{Yin+Li:2011}, \citet{Kim+Xing:2012} and
\citet{Li+Nan+Zhu:2015}. This makes them useful for examining the practical
consequences of marginal sign restrictions. The partial-coefficient signs are
unknown, so this application cannot verify the structural assumptions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:gate}. We evaluate prediction and resampling stability
of the complete pipeline without treating marginal signs as conditional
coefficient truth.

\subsection{Data and evaluation scope} \label{sec:eqtl-data}

The cross of two yeast strains provides $T=112$ segregants
\citep{Brem+Kruglyak:2005}. Membership in the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG)
mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway gives $N=64$
genes, rather than the 54-gene subset used in
\citet{Yin+Li:2011}. The annotations include pheromone response,
filamentation, high-osmolarity response, cell-wall integrity and a sporulation
(Smk1) category.
The original panel contains 3244 markers. The primary predictive comparison
uses the nested protocol below.

\subsection{Repeated nested validation}\label{sec:eqtl-nested}

The protocol starts from all 3,244 markers and the 64 genes
selected by the prespecified MAPK annotation, and uses three repeats of
three outer folds with three inner folds (seed 20261024). Each inner
training fold independently recomputes linkage-disequilibrium (LD) thinning at absolute correlation
$0.92$, marginal screening at $p\leq0.01$ in at least two genes, centering,
scaling, Ward clustering and gate signs. The selected procedure is refitted
using only the outer training observations. Outer outcomes are used only
for prediction assessment and the separate descriptive diagnostics below.
Outer training sets retain 136--194 markers and form three or four response
clusters.

All observations receive equal weight. The date-score threshold $\tau=2$,
final-merge cut fraction $0.7$, mixing parameter $\alpha=0.5$ for HCGL/FCGL,
zero prior target and nonadaptive weights $W=1$ are fixed in advance.
These are fixed reference settings for matched comparisons, and neighboring
settings are assessed in Section~\ref{sec:eqtl-sensitivity}.
Within each inner fold, each method has its own zero-solution Karush--Kuhn--Tucker (KKT) penalty
scale $\lambda_{\max}$, respecting its groups and restrictions.
The candidate fractions are $\{1,0.3,0.1,0.03\}\lambda_{\max}$.
We minimize pooled inner validation squared error in training-standardized
response units. Every outer fit selects fraction $0.3$, an interior grid
point, and the corresponding numerical penalties differ across methods.

The eight methods comprise ordinary unconstrained Lasso, singleton-gated Lasso,
unconstrained HCGL, singleton-gated HCGL, pooled HCGL with exclusion or release,
and unconstrained or pooled-excluded FCGL. Release retains the half-space for a
passed gate but leaves a failed gate unrestricted. The four HCGL variants share
the row-norm penalty, so the singleton-versus-pooled HCGL comparison isolates
the gate's pooling. The unrestricted FCGL comparison isolates restrictions in
the coupled penalty. Both additional baselines use the same outer/inner splits,
training-only preprocessing and penalty-fraction grid.

For gene $k$ and repeat $r$, predictions from all outer test folds are pooled
before computing
\[
 R^2_{kr}=1-\frac{\sum_i(y_{ik}-\widehat y^{(-f(i),r)}_{ik})^2}
 {\sum_i(y_{ik}-\overline y^{(-f(i),r)}_k)^2},
\]
where the baseline mean is learned from the corresponding outer training
fold. We report the median and mean over genes, and pooled $R^2$, which
sums both numerator and denominator over genes before taking their ratio.
The pooled score gives more weight to genes with larger baseline squared
variation in their original expression units.

so it does not bound every remaining pairwise correlation. Among outer-training
selected markers, the mean fold median absolute correlation is $0.088$, the mean
95th percentile is $0.306$, and the mean maximum is $0.969$. On average $2.56\%$
of pairs exceed $0.5$ and $0.89\%$ exceed $0.8$. Residual predictor dependence
therefore remains relevant to marginal-versus-conditional sign disagreement.

\subsection{Application sensitivity}\label{sec:eqtl-sensitivity}

Ten predeclared one-factor neighbors vary LD pruning, outcome screening,
Ward cut, gate threshold or mixing while retaining the same inner/outer splits.
Only the penalty is tuned, and outer outcomes do not select a setting. Pooled
exclusion has lower median-gene $R^2$ than unconstrained and released-gate HCGL
in every repeat at every setting, with mean paired losses of $0.020$--$0.069$
against unconstrained HCGL. The loss changes with the cut and threshold, and
release stays close to unconstrained HCGL without establishing equivalence.
No outer fit selects a penalty endpoint. Online Resource~1,
Sections~\ref{app:eqtl-settings} and~\ref{app:eqtl-sensitivity}, reports all
settings, results and same-fold configuration stability. Held-out marginal
coherence exceeds a size-matched random-partition reference, but does not
validate conditional directions or biological pathways (Online Resource~1,
Section~\ref{sec:eqtl-heldout}).

Separately, we compare independent-cell and date-score variance on the same
nine outer splits, holding preprocessing, groups and numerical penalties fixed.
Switching to date scores newly excludes $32.71\%$ of all coefficient cells, and
the median relative loading-matrix change is $53.62\%$ for HCGL and $18.10\%$
for FCGL. Standardized held-out mean squared error increases by $1.39\%$ and
$0.56\%$, respectively. These are fixed-setting sensitivity results without
variance-specific retuning, not replacements for the nested comparisons.
Online Resource~1, Section~\ref{app:variance-sensitivity}, gives the protocol,
full scores and synthetic application controls.

\section{Computation and reproducibility}\label{sec:reproduction}

Conditional on the partition, signs and weights, the HCGL objective of
Equation~\eqref{eq:sgl-sign} separates across responses, so the $N$ constrained
fits can run in parallel, whereas the FCGL block norm couples each cluster's
responses. The solver is CVXPY with MOSEK for the fitted-estimator comparisons
and the yeast fits, and CLARABEL for the synthetic software-example fits and for
independent reference solves of selected objectives. A valid MOSEK license is
needed for full reproduction. Measured path timings across four dimensions are
reported in Online Resource~1, Section~\ref{app:scaling}, separately from
nested-validation cost. A fresh Windows CPython 3.12.7 environment with an
installed \texttt{factorlasso} 1.0.5 wheel reproduced all 2,680 primary
synthetic fits, 16,800 sensitivity refits and the nested eQTL coefficients and
performance metrics. This is clean-environment reproduction on the same host,
not independent-laboratory or cross-platform replication. Online Resource~2
supplies the exact source, frozen inputs, fitted outputs, environment pins and

all-failed infinite-threshold endpoint correction as a separately hashed

\section{Discussion} \label{sec:discussion}

% [CLAUDE-DRAFT begin: reframed opening]
The paper shows when pooling marginal sign evidence across response clusters
helps a subsequent sparse regression and when it harms. Pooling $m$ responses
multiplies the precision of a marginal slope by
$\sqrt{m/\{1+(m-1)\rho_e\}}$, and the gate then retains the correct direction
on every nonzero coefficient under Proposition~\ref{prop:gate}, whose
marginal-sign condition Lemma~\ref{lem:leakage} reduces to a bound on
coefficient-weighted predictor covariances. The gain is conditional on
coherence. At matched null retention the learned-pooled gate recovers $0.030$
more correct active signs than the singleton gate when cluster members share
supports and directions, and $0.066$ and $0.134$ fewer under mixed directions
and partial supports. The fitted comparisons repeat this pattern. Pooled
exclusion raises coherent-design test $R^2$ from $0.614$ to $0.626$ and is
level with cooperative Lasso, unconstrained FCGL and UniLasso, and on the yeast
data it lowers median-gene $R^2$ from $0.073$ to $0.035$ while release leaves
it at $0.073$. Releasing a failed gate is therefore the safe default, and
exclusion pays only when the grouping is coherent.
% [CLAUDE-DRAFT end]

Gate failure can either exclude a coefficient or leave it unrestricted. Those
actions answer different questions. Exclusion screens the support, while
unrestricted failure declines to constrain the direction. Their consequences
must be evaluated through fitted-coefficient error, selected support, sign
coverage and prediction, rather than through low flip rates alone. The first
simulation family finds small, uncertain gains in its baseline and a clear loss
from exclusion in the correlated case. The recognizable-group study recovers
nontrivial groups and finds a coherent-group prediction gain over the
corresponding jointly tuned unconstrained HCGL procedure. Joint tuning can
change the grouping and does not isolate a pure gate effect. Pure-L1 mixing is
selected in 32--40 of 40 pooled-exclusion replications, so those comparisons do
not establish an advantage for the group-penalty term. The nested eQTL analysis
favours singleton gating or releasing failed gates over pooled exclusion within
the same HCGL penalty, and its high direction agreement is conditional on a
small overlap of selected cells.

For prediction, the cut and gate threshold belong inside the same training-only
selection procedure as the penalty. The comparison set should include the
unconstrained objective, singleton gating and failed-gate release. A fixed
threshold such as $2$ is a screening setting, not a validated significance level.
Sensitivity to cut, threshold, variance convention and numerical zero
classification should accompany interpretation of selected coefficients. The
paired variance comparison shows that retaining within-date dependence can
materially change loadings without improving prediction, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:precision}(b) shows why the independent-cell convention cannot
be trusted when marginal residuals are correlated. The package defaults are
unchanged, and the date-score convention in this study is an explicit choice.

The limitations are these. The on-support result is fixed-dimensional and
assumes a supplied threshold sequence. It is not a selection theorem for the
fitted estimator, a calibrated multiple-testing procedure, or a consistency
result for the joint cross-validation rule. Marginal signs can disagree with
partial signs, and no gate statistic corrects that. The simulation families
are small, the grids are finite, and the gains are confined to coherent
structures. Repeated splits of one yeast cross measure sensitivity to
splitting, not replication across independent biological samples, and
marginal-sign agreement there is descriptive. The eQTL analysis fixes the cut
and threshold that the simulations tune. The finite-span, missing-history and
serially dependent extensions require additional theory. Reliable external
groups or independently validated marginal directions remain the most direct
settings in which to investigate a practical benefit from pooling.
